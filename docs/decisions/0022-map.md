# 0022 · Plasmie's map: the vault as a living network, drawn natively

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Area:** Design, architecture

## Context
The owner asked for "a cute graph flow like Obsidian's graph view but cuter, more
interactible, and Plasmie in the middle; zoom in and out, hover to see a node". The name
already says what such a map should look like: Plasmie comes from *plasmodium*, a slime
mold that grows a network of veins and passes nutrients along it
([0014](0014-brand-plasmie.md)). A vault is a few hundred curated notes and a couple of
thousand wikilinks, and the map should stay smooth into the low thousands. The app is native
SwiftUI, macOS 13+, built with swiftc (no Xcode, no packages).

## Decision
1. **Engine: `plasmie-graph.py`** (stdlib, read-only, caches nothing) prints the wiki as
   JSON: nodes (vault-relative path, title, type, tags, date, link count, hub flag, two-line
   excerpt) and undirected edges. Wikilinks resolve the way Obsidian does: by file name
   (case-insensitive, folder prefix ignored), else by title; `|alias`, `#heading` and
   `^block` point at the note; missing targets and attachments are dropped. Maps of content
   (`type: moc|index` or under `wiki/mocs/`) are hubs. Every displayable string passes
   `scrub.redact(emails=True)` on its final form, before truncation; no absolute paths are
   emitted. It takes ~0.1–0.16 s on a real vault.
2. **Rendering: one SwiftUI `Canvas`**, not a view per note, inside a `TimelineView` that is
   paused whenever nothing moves. Everything of one look is batched into one `Path` (all
   veins; each kind × freshness bucket of blobs), so a frame is a few dozen fills. The real
   `PlasmieView` is overlaid at the world origin (frozen while the map sleeps). Input (wheel,
   pinch, hover, drag) comes from a thin AppKit view, since SwiftUI on macOS 13 can't reach
   all of it.
3. **Layout: a Swift force simulation** in the d3-force tradition: Barnes-Hut repulsion,
   degree-biased spring edges, springs from Plasmie (pinned at the origin) to the maps,
   gentle gravity, grid-bucketed collision. Alpha cools over ~300 ticks; once settled the map
   stops ticking (0% CPU idle). Initial positions come from each note's path hash, and ticks
   (not wall time) drive it, so the same vault always settles into the same map.
4. **Look: pink is Plasmie's alone.** The ground is a neutral (`mapGround`); notes are minute
   graphite blobs (2–5 pt; maps 5.5–6.5 pt with a ring; size by the square root of links).
   Accents reuse tokens that already mean something: sparkle gold for decisions, sweat blue
   for gotchas, leaf green for patterns, ink for how-tos. Veins are curved, tapered,
   translucent ink, thicker only near Plasmie. The one lit effect is a faint sparkle-gold glow
   on lit veins and on the nutrient pulses that walk downhill to Plasmie.
5. **Accessibility:** the canvas is one labelled element. The notes list (toolbar, or open
   automatically when VoiceOver is on) lists every note by type as buttons that open it,
   with a "Show on map" action; hovering a row lights the note on the map. Reduce Motion: no
   pulses or breathing, the layout arrives settled, every move is a 0.15 s ease.
6. **Entry points:** the Plasmie window's Open group ("Map", ⌘G), the notch's right-click
   menu ("Open map"), and `--map` at launch: a map-only viewer (no migrations, jobs, notch,
   hotkey or status polling) used for render checks.

## Alternatives rejected
- A WebView with d3 or sigma: a browser engine and JS bundle for one window, and a second
  rendering stack to keep on brand.
- SpriteKit: heavier, and its text and accessibility are worse than SwiftUI's.
- A view per note: doesn't scale past a few hundred.
- Caching the graph on disk: it is fast enough to compute on open, and a cache is one more
  copy of note text.

## Consequences
- Measured on Apple silicon with a real vault (411 notes, 1,888 links): 120 fps while
  animating (the display's cap), ~1.4 ms to record a frame, physics ~0.3 ms a tick while
  settling, 0% CPU once asleep (~8 s after the last touch).
- The engine is the only reader of note content; the app never opens vault files for the map.
